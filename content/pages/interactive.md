# Interactive video generators: you type, the video answers

An **interactive video generator** closes the loop between watching and doing. You type a direction, cast a vote, or click a choice — and the video _changes_: the model generates new footage that incorporates what you did, live. No branches pre-filmed in advance, no "choose option A or B" recorded months ago. The story computes its next scene because of you, seconds after you weigh in.

That's the clearest way interactive AI video differs from everything that came before. Old interactive video was a maze of pre-recorded clips; interactive AI video is a _conversation with footage_.

## What is an interactive video generator?

Three properties define the format:

1. **The video is generated, not selected.** There is no clip library behind it — a video model produces each frame as it plays.
2. **Input redirects the stream.** Text, votes, or actions change what the next frames show. The model isn't playing a video at you; it's answering you.
3. **State persists.** Characters and story carry forward, so your choices have consequences later in the stream.

When those three run in a live, always-on channel, you get the strangest new feeling on the internet: television that listens.

## The three levels of interactive AI video

### Level 1: Vote on what happens next

The entry level. You watch the show and, at each beat, the chat votes between directions — the islanders search the cave or repair the raft; the cast confronts the intruder or hides. [Infinite Slop](/channel/infinite-slop) runs its endless reality show this way, rewarding contributors with karma and a cash prize pool ($8,500 at last check). [Renoise Live](/channel/renoise-live) makes chat the weather: four islanders, one storm, your call.

### Level 2: Type to steer, scene by scene

More agency: you don't just pick from options, you _write_ them. On [fal.live](/channel/fal-live), anyone can propose a direction in chat; the room votes; the winning prompt becomes the next scene, rendered live within seconds. The channel's premise — written once as its resident Director prompt — keeps every suggestion inside the show's world. The craft of writing those premises is covered in [the AI video director guide](/director).

### Level 3: Run the show yourself

The deepest interaction is ownership. Hosted studios like [AITV](/channel/aitv) and [Retake.tv](/channel/retake-tv) launch your own AI streamer in minutes; open-source stacks like [Infinite TV](/channel/infinite-tv), [SlopTV](/channel/sloptv), [AIRI](/channel/airi), and [AITuber Kit](/channel/aituber-kit) hand you the whole station. You define the world, wire the audience in, and keep it on air. Start here: [how to launch an AI channel](/creators).

## Quick answers

### How is interactive AI video different from a chatbot with a face?

A chatbot answers with text or speech. Interactive AI video answers with new _footage_: your input is turned into the next scene of a running video stream, generated live by a video model.

### Does my vote actually change the video?

On audience-voting channels, yes — the top-voted suggestion becomes the instruction for the next scene, and you watch it render within seconds. On [Infinite Slop](/channel/infinite-slop) and [Renoise Live](/channel/renoise-live), the show is visibly built from viewer decisions.

### Can one person steer, or does the crowd decide?

On live channels the crowd decides — proposals go to a vote and the winner screens. Running your own channel (or a self-hosted stack) gives a single director full control.
